Boehm GC benchmarks, November 2026

What Acton's bdwgc fork gives LavinMQ

The mark stack fix already removes the long pauses. On top of it, Acton's fork leaves pauses where they are and costs 16% more memory. The fork's one large gain, 3 to 4 times faster allocation from several threads, comes from a 2-line lock ordering change we can apply to 8.2.12.

Answer

Four results

±5%

Pauses and GC time, fork vs fix

With 2,000 connections, idle or under full load, the fork's pause p50, pause max and GC time per minute stay within ±5% of fix. stock 8.2.12 pauses 3 to 4 times longer than either.

+16%

Peak RSS with the fork

About 100 MB more in every full-load run, also with GC_UNMAP_THRESHOLD=1. RSS follows libgc's heap size, which the fork sizes larger during the start-up burst.

-34%

GC time at full message rate

34 to 23 ms per second at 1M msg/s. libgc is only 4.4% of broker CPU there (3.1% with the fork), so throughput moves within noise.

3.3×

Allocation from 4 threads, 2-line backport

667 to 205 ns per step with fix-fl; the fork does 173. LavinMQ runs one mutator thread today, so this pays off with Crystal's multi-threaded scheduler.

Recommendation

What to ship

  1. Ship fix in the 84codes/crystal libgc: the global mark stack patch with a 32,768-entry initial stack. It is the whole pause win (B2, B3 below).
  2. Add bdwgc-8.2.12-fl-builder-unlock-order.patch to the same build: 2 lines, no change single-threaded, 3.3× for parallel allocation. Propose both patches upstream (upstream master has the same lock order).
  3. Don't adopt the fork for now: no gain on LavinMQ's workload and 16% more RSS. Re-measure prefetch on push on a machine with 16 or more cores before dismissing it for large deployments.

Root cause

The allocator lock convoy

A thread whose thread-local free list runs dry calls GC_generic_malloc_many. Under the allocator lock it takes a heap block, registers itself as a free-list builder by incrementing GC_fl_builder_count under the mark lock, and then builds the free list outside the allocator lock.

8.2.12 releases the allocator lock while it still holds the mark lock. The next thread waiting for the allocator lock gets it and then blocks on the mark lock, and so on down the line. With 4 threads allocating, 31% of all CPU samples sit in futex wakes.

Releasing the mark lock first is safe: only the allocator lock holder increments the count, and the collector waits for the count to reach zero while holding the allocator lock, so nobody can be waiting at that point. Acton's 27c37207 makes the count atomic and, in its fallback path, uses exactly this order. The atomic part is not what matters: acton-noatomicfl is as fast.
